## Why a focus room needs a few shared habits

A virtual coworking room only works if everyone can concentrate. Nobody wants to read a rulebook before they start, and most good etiquette is common sense. Still, a few shared habits make the difference between a room that feels calm and one that feels like an unplanned meeting.

The guidelines below apply to most online focus rooms, including MySession's scheduled sessions and always-open rooms. Individual hosts can set stricter rules, so check the session card before you join. If you are new to the format, the overview of [how virtual coworking rooms work](/virtual-coworking) is a good place to start.

## Before you join

**Read the room rules.** Session cards on MySession show a badge when a room requires a camera, requires screen sharing, accepts either, or has public chat turned off. If a rule does not fit your situation today, choose another room rather than joining and ignoring it.

**Have your task ready.** Open the document, close what you do not need, and know your first step. Joining with a plan means the first few minutes go to work, not to deciding what to work on.

**Check your audio and video once.** A quick look at the pre-join preview avoids a loud echo or a camera pointed at the ceiling.

## During the intentions stage

**Keep it short.** One sentence is enough: "Draft the introduction to my report" or "Clear the receipts folder." Other people want to start too.

**Share the outcome, not the private details.** You do not need to name a client, an employer, or a sensitive document. Our guide to [writing a focus session intention](/blog/how-to-write-a-focus-session-intention) has examples that are specific without revealing anything private.

**Listen or read, then let it go.** You do not need to respond to everyone's intention. A quick reaction in chat is friendly; a long discussion is better saved for the break.

## During focus blocks

**Mute your microphone.** Even a quiet keyboard, a fan, or background voices can be distracting through someone else's headphones. Unmute only if the room's host invites it.

**Keep chat for essentials.** A short note like "back in 5" helps the host. Ongoing conversations during a focus block pull everyone out of their work. If public chat is off, the host has decided to protect the block completely.

**Stay in frame or step away cleanly.** If the room uses video and you need a few minutes away, turning the camera off is less distracting than an empty chair or a moving background. If you are leaving for good, a short goodbye in chat is enough.

**Do not screen-share by accident.** Only share your screen when the room's rules ask for it, and check what is visible before you do.

## During breaks and check-ins

**Use breaks as breaks.** Stand up, refill your water, look away from the screen. Breaks are part of the structure, not lost time.

**Report progress honestly and briefly.** "Finished the outline, stuck on section two" is a great check-in. You do not need to have completed everything, and nobody is grading your output.

**Be kind about other people's progress.** A short "nice work" is welcome. Advice that nobody asked for usually is not.

## Privacy and respect

Treat other participants the way you would in a quiet library. Do not record or screenshot people without their permission, and do not comment on someone's home, appearance, or background. If someone breaks the room's rules or makes the space feel unsafe, use the room's reporting tools rather than arguing in chat.

Remember that you control what you share. You can keep your camera angled away from private papers, use a generic intention, and leave at any time.
