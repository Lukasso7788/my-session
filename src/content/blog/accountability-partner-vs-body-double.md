## Two kinds of support that often get mixed up

"Accountability partner" and "body double" are sometimes used as if they mean the same thing. They overlap, but they solve different problems. Knowing which one you need saves you from asking a friend for weekly goal reviews when what you really need is someone to start working at the same time as you, or the reverse.

In short: an **accountability partner** helps you keep commitments over days and weeks. A **body double** helps you start and stay with a task right now.

## What an accountability partner does

An accountability partner is someone you report to. You tell them what you plan to do this week, and later you tell them whether it happened. The relationship usually runs over time: a friend you message every Monday, a colleague you swap goals with, or a small group that reviews progress at the end of each month.

This works when the problem is direction or follow-through. If you have long-term goals that keep slipping, a regular check with another person makes the slippage visible. A good partner asks useful questions: what got in the way, what is the next step, and is the goal still the right one?

What an accountability partner usually does **not** do is sit with you while you work. You can have a very good weekly check-in and still spend Tuesday afternoon unable to open the document.

## What a body double does

A body double is someone who works on their own task while you work on yours. They do not review your goals or judge your progress. Their presence marks a shared start and a shared finish, which makes it easier to begin and to come back after a distraction.

This works when the problem is starting or staying put. Many people know exactly what they need to do and still cannot begin. Shared presence turns "I should do this" into "the session has started, so I am doing this now." Our [guide to body doubling](/guides/what-is-body-doubling) explains the method and walks through a first session.

A body double can be a friend on a video call, a housemate at the same table, or a room of strangers in a [live body doubling session](/body-doubling). It does not need to be the same person each time.

## Side by side

| | Accountability partner | Body double |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Time frame | Days, weeks, or months | One work block |
| Main question | "Did you do what you said?" | "Are you working now?" |
| Contact | Scheduled check-ins | Shared presence during work |
| Needs to know your goals | Usually, in some detail | Only a one-line intention, if any |
| Best for | Direction and follow-through | Starting and staying on task |
| Typical format | Messages, calls, regular reviews | Live session or room |

## When you need one, the other, or both

**You need an accountability partner** if you start tasks easily but lose track of bigger goals, or if weeks pass without progress on something important that no one else is waiting for.

**You need a body double** if you know the next step but keep postponing it, if working alone makes the day feel shapeless, or if you lose focus after a few minutes without anyone around. The [body doubling for procrastination](/body-doubling-for-procrastination) page covers this situation in more detail.

**You may benefit from both** if your weekly plan is clear on Sunday but nothing happens on Monday. A simple combination: agree on the week's priorities with a partner, then book or join body doubling sessions for the specific tasks on that list.

## How MySession fits in

MySession is built for the body doubling side. You join a live room, write a short intention, work through a shared timeline, and check in at the end. Rooms do not track long-term goals for you, and other participants are not there to coach you.

You can still add a light layer of accountability. Keep your session intentions and tasks visible in the room, then review them at the end of the week. Patterns show up quickly: which tasks you started, which you avoided, and which session lengths worked. The [remote-work accountability routine](/blog/remote-work-accountability-without-meetings) shows one way to turn those notes into a weekly review without adding meetings.

If you want one specific person to expect you, a 1:1 session or a private room link is closer to a partner arrangement. For most daily work, though, the simplest test is this: if your problem is starting, find a body double first.
