## Two ways to show up

Online focus sessions generally come in two shapes. A **scheduled session** starts at a fixed time, often with a host and a planned timeline. A **drop-in room** is open whenever you arrive, and its structure repeats so you can start at the next focus block. Both use shared presence to help you begin. They differ in when commitment happens: before the session, or at the moment you join.

Neither format is better in general. The right choice depends on what usually stops you from working. This guide helps you match the format to that obstacle.

## What a scheduled session gives you

A scheduled session turns a vague plan into an appointment. When you book "Tuesday, 9:00," you have made a decision in advance, and the start time does the motivating for you. That is useful when you know you will otherwise find a reason to postpone.

Scheduled sessions also tend to have a clear arc: a welcome, a moment to share intentions, focus and break blocks, and a check-in at the end. Hosts often run the same slot every week, so the same people come back and the room feels familiar.

The cost is timing. If you feel ready at 10:17, you may have to wait for the next session. If you miss the start, you join in the middle of a block. And a fixed calendar slot does not help on days when your schedule changes at the last minute.

Many platforms use this model. As of October 2026, Flow Club and Caveday both run hosted or guided sessions you reserve in advance; Caveday also offers a 24/7 Focus Lounge alongside its scheduled Caves.

## What a drop-in room gives you

A drop-in room removes the wait. You open it when you are ready and start with the next focus block. That matters on days when motivation shows up briefly and disappears if you have to schedule it.

Drop-in rooms suit irregular days, short tasks, and late-night or early-morning work. They also make it easy to stay for one more round when things are going well.

The cost is commitment. Nothing pulls you into a drop-in room except your own decision, so it is easier to keep putting it off. Who is in the room also varies: an always-open room is always available, but it is not always full.

## Match the format to the obstacle

| If your usual problem is... | Try |
| --- | --- |
| "I'll start later" turns into never | Scheduled session |
| You feel ready at unpredictable times | Drop-in room |
| Your days are fragmented by meetings or caregiving | Drop-in room between commitments |
| You want a weekly routine with familiar people | Recurring scheduled session |
| You need a long, protected block | Scheduled session with prepared materials |
| You have one quick task to get over the line | Drop-in room, one short block |

You can also combine them: book a scheduled session for the hard task you are most likely to avoid, and use drop-in rooms for everything else.

## How this works on MySession

The [sessions page](/sessions) shows both formats. Group sessions have a start time, a host, and a timeline that you can preview on the session card. Always-open rooms (also called Infinite rooms) run a repeating structure, so you can enter at any hour and pick up the next focus block. Session cards also show room rules such as camera requirements, so you know what to expect before you join.

If neither feels right yet, try the same task once in each format during the same week. Note which session you actually started on time and which one you finished. That small experiment usually answers the question better than any general advice.

## Prepare the same way either way

The format changes when you start; it does not change what makes a session productive. Before you join, write one checkable outcome, close what you will not need, and choose a block length that fits the task.
