## Why host a session

Hosting a virtual coworking session sounds like more work than joining one, but a good host mostly sets the structure and then gets on with their own task. Hosting is useful when you want a time slot that fits your routine, a room with rules you prefer, or a regular session for friends, classmates, or colleagues.

This guide walks through planning a session, choosing room rules, and running the welcome, focus, and check-in stages. The examples use MySession, where both the Free and Pro plans include hosting, but the same principles apply to any shared focus room.

## Plan the session before you publish it

**Pick a clear title and description.** People choose sessions from a list, so say what the room is for: "Morning writing block," "Evening study sprint," or "Deep work, cameras optional." A one-line description sets expectations: who the room is for and how quiet it will be.

**Choose a time people can actually make.** A recurring slot at the same time each week builds a regular group faster than one-off sessions. Leave a few minutes before and after for people to join and wrap up.

**Choose the rhythm.** MySession's session builder includes 15/3, 25/5, and 50/10 presets and lets you repeat the cycle several times. If you are unsure, start with 25/5 for mixed groups or 50/10 for people doing longer work. Our article on [group Pomodoro sessions](/blog/group-pomodoro-sessions) explains how to match the interval to the task.

## Build a simple timeline

A timeline tells everyone what happens next, so you do not need to manage it out loud. A dependable structure looks like this:

| Stage | Length | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Welcome | 2-3 minutes | People arrive, settle in, check audio |
| Intentions | 3-5 minutes | Everyone writes or says one goal |
| Focus | 25-50 minutes | Quiet, independent work |
| Break | 5-10 minutes | Step away from the screen |
| Focus | 25-50 minutes | Second block, if the session is long |
| Check-in | 3-5 minutes | Share what changed and the next step |

MySession's timeline editor supports welcome, intentions, focus, break, check-in, recap, and celebrate blocks, plus custom blocks, so you can adjust the order and length. You can save a timeline as a template and reuse it for the next session.

## Set the room rules

Decide on rules before people join, not during the session. On MySession you can:

- **Set a maximum number of participants** if you want a smaller, quieter room.
- **Choose who can join:** a public session listed on the [sessions page](/sessions), or a private session shared only by link.
- **Require a camera, require screen sharing, or accept either one**, if the room depends on visible presence.
- **Turn off public chat** for focus-heavy rooms where messages would distract.

These rules appear as badges on the session card, so participants know what to expect. Strict rules are not better by default. A camera requirement can help a committed group, but it also excludes people who cannot use video. Our guide on [body doubling with the camera on or off](/blog/body-doubling-camera-on-or-off) discusses that trade-off.

## Run the welcome and intentions

Keep the opening short and warm. Greet people as they arrive, then invite intentions: "Share one goal for this block in chat or out loud. Keep it to one sentence." Model it yourself with a specific example such as "I'm drafting two pages of my report." If someone writes a vague intention, do not correct them; a good example from you does more.

Avoid turning the opening into a discussion. People came to work, and a two-minute delay feels long when you are ready to start.

## Stay quiet during focus blocks

During focus, the best thing a host can do is work. Keep your microphone muted and stay off chat except for brief, necessary notes. If someone joins late, a short welcome message is enough. The guide to [virtual coworking etiquette](/blog/virtual-coworking-etiquette) lists habits you can share in your description so new participants know the norms.

## Close with a check-in

At the end, ask each person what changed and what they will do next. Celebrate progress, not output: "finished the outline" and "finally opened the file" both count. Thank people for coming, and mention the next session if you host on a schedule.

## Keep it sustainable

Host at a time you would work anyway, so the session supports your own routine rather than competing with it. Start with one session a week, keep the same timeline, and adjust one thing at a time based on who returns.
